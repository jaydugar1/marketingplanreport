Plain-English answers about where the numbers in the Account Marketing Plan Report come from, how current they are, and how to read them. Click any question to expand it.

Data & freshness

1. Where does the data come from?

Short answer: everything comes straight from Salesforce. Nobody types numbers into this report, and no spreadsheet feeds it.

The report reads two things in Salesforce:

Salesforce objectWhat it contributes
AccountThe list of clients and their basics: client name, whether they are Client – Live or Client – Implementing, region (used to spot Channel Partners), a backup eligible-employee count, and the coverage details used to identify Cancer-Only clients.
Account Marketing Plan
(a custom Salesforce object)		One record per client per plan period. This is where the 2027 information lives: Client Tier, Marketing Channel & Approval Status, ID Card Approval Status, Testing Program, eligible employees, and the campaign start/end dates.

Behind the scenes, a scheduled job asks Salesforce for anything that has changed, updates a small data file, and rebuilds this page. The page itself never connects to Salesforce, so visitors do not need Salesforce access.

Salesforce→Daily refresh job→Data file→This report

What this means for you: if something looks wrong, the fix is almost always to correct the record in Salesforce (see question 12). The report will pick it up on the next refresh.

Is Monday.com used? No. Some Salesforce fields are copied to Monday for the marketing team's workflow, but this report reads Salesforce only, so the two should agree but are not linked to each other here.

2. How often is it refreshed?

Short answer: once a day, on weekdays, early in the morning (around 7:15 AM). Changes made in Salesforce during the day show up the next morning.

  • Monday–Friday: one refresh each morning.
  • Weekends: no refresh. Anything changed Friday afternoon through Sunday appears Monday morning.
  • Same-day changes: if a client approves a campaign at 2 PM and the record is updated in Salesforce, the report will still show the old status until the next morning's refresh.
  • If a refresh is missed: the previous version stays up. It is never blank, just older. The job runs from a desktop app, so a day when the computer is off or asleep can delay it until the app is next opened.

What about the "page reloads itself" behavior? The page quietly reloads every 15 minutes, which is only so that a long-open browser tab picks up the latest published version. It does not mean the data is refreshed every 15 minutes; the underlying data only changes when the daily refresh runs.

Only records that changed since the last pull are fetched each day. After each pull, the job also compares its client and plan counts against Salesforce. If they do not match, it re-pulls everything; if they still do not match, it keeps the previous version instead of publishing something wrong.

3. How do I know how current the numbers are?

Look at the top-right of the report: "Snapshot pulled <date, time>". That is the moment the data was last pulled from Salesforce.

  • Pulled this morning (weekday): normal.
  • Pulled yesterday morning: you are looking at yesterday's data; today's refresh has not run or published yet.
  • Pulled several weekdays ago: a refresh is likely failing. Let the report owner know.

The time is shown in your own computer's time zone.

4. The numbers don't match what I see in Salesforce right now. Why?

The most common reasons, in order of likelihood:

  1. Timing. You changed the record after this morning's refresh. It will appear tomorrow.
  2. A blank counts as Pending. If a Marketing Channel, ID Card, or Testing Program field is empty in Salesforce, the report shows Pending (see question 8).
  3. Different grouping. The report applies rules that a Salesforce list view may not: channel partners, off-cycle plans, and Cancer-Only clients are pulled out of the Tier, Implementing and Entire BoB tabs (see question 9).
  4. Which plan record counts. A client can have several plan records (for example a 2026 one and a 2027 one). Only the 2027 plan is used (question 7).
  5. Account type. Only accounts typed Client – Live or Client – Implementing are included. A client moved to "Terming" or "Former Client" drops out.

Reading the report

5. Which clients are included?

Every Salesforce Account whose type is Client – Live or Client – Implementing. That list is then split into the tabs along the top (Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, Entire BoB, Implementing, Cancer-Only, Channel Partners, Off-Cycle).

Prospects, disqualified accounts, terming clients and former clients are not included. There is one hand-added exception, explained in question 11.

6. What does "No 2027 Plan" mean?

The client is active, but there is no 2027 Account Marketing Plan record for them in Salesforce yet. Rather than hiding these clients, the report keeps them in every count and labels their statuses No 2027 Plan, so you can see who still needs a plan created.

These clients also have no tier yet (tier comes from the 2027 plan), so they show up in Entire BoB and Implementing (if applicable) but not in Tier 1–3.

The "x / y have a 2027 marketing plan started" figure in the summary strip tells you how many clients in the current tab do have a plan.

7. How does the report decide which plan record is the "2027" one?

A plan counts as 2027 if either:

  • its start date falls between Nov 1, 2026 and Oct 31, 2027, or
  • its end date falls in calendar year 2027.

If a client has more than one that qualifies, plans starting in that Nov 2026–Oct 2027 window win; after that, the one with the latest start date is used. This is how clients on a non-calendar plan year (for example Oct 2026–Sep 2027) are still matched to the 2027 report.

9. Why is a client in one tab but not another? (How are the tabs built?)
TabWho is in it
Tier 1 / 2 / 3Clients whose 2027 plan has that Client Tier. Excludes Channel Partners, Off-Cycle plans and Cancer-Only clients.
Implementing ClientsClients still being implemented. A client with a tier appears here and in its Tier tab; that overlap is intentional. Same exclusions as the tiers.
Entire BoBThe whole book of business, with Channel Partners, Off-Cycle and Cancer-Only clients removed.
Cancer-OnlyClients whose only covered benefit is the cancer program (see question 10). Kept separate: never in Tier, Implementing or Off-Cycle.
Channel PartnersAccounts flagged as a Channel Partner (Region = Channel Partner). No other filtering, so one can also appear under Cancer-Only or Off-Cycle.
Off-Cycle ClientsClients whose 2027 plan starts outside the normal January cycle: start date Mar 1 – Nov 1, 2027, or Nov 1 – Dec 31, 2026. The Reason column shows the start date.

If a client seems to be missing from Tier 1–3, the usual causes are: no 2027 plan yet (no tier), the plan is off-cycle, they are a Channel Partner, or they are Cancer-Only.

10. What is a "Cancer-Only" client?

A client whose Salesforce record shows the cancer program (CCD) as Voluntary and every one of the other 18 benefit categories as Not Covered — surgery types such as bariatric, cardiac, spine, joint replacement, GI, GYN, urology and so on, plus colonoscopies and infusions.

They are shown on their own tab because the ID Card question does not apply to them (that card is hidden there) and mixing them into the tiers would distort the percentages.

11. What does the asterisk (*) next to a client name mean?

It marks a manual override: a client who really is active, but whose Salesforce record is set up differently from the usual pattern, so it would not be picked up automatically. Hover the asterisk for the reason.

Today this applies to Curative (Salesforce type "Active Partner", included as a Tier 1 live client). These overrides are written into the report itself, not into Salesforce, and should be removed once the Salesforce record is corrected.

A handful of internal test accounts are also deliberately excluded from all counts.

12. Something in the report is wrong. How do I fix it?
  1. Find the client in Salesforce and open its Account Marketing Plan record for 2027.
  2. Correct the field that is wrong (tier, channel & approval status, ID card status, testing program, or eligible employees).
  3. Wait for the next morning's refresh. Check the "Snapshot pulled" time to confirm the new data is live.

Do not expect edits to the report page itself to stick; the page is rebuilt from Salesforce every day. If the Salesforce data is right but the report still looks off, contact the report owner — it may be a rule that needs updating (for example, a new status option or a changed off-cycle window).

13. What's the difference between "% of EEs" and "# of Clients"?
  • # of Clients counts each client once. A 300-employee company and a 300,000-employee company count the same.
  • % of EEs weights each client by its number of eligible employees (EEs), so large clients move the bars more. It answers "what share of the eligible population has approved?"

The EE number comes from the 2027 plan record; if it is blank there, the Account's eligible-employee count is used instead. Some clients have neither. They are left out of the % of EEs math (otherwise they would count as zero) but still counted in # of Clients. The orange note in the summary strip tells you how many clients are missing an EE count in the current tab; those rows show "—" in the EEs column.

Access & upkeep

14. Who can see this, and is it safe to share?

The report contains client names, employee counts and approval statuses, which are internal business information. Share the link only with people who would normally have access to this information in Salesforce, and do not forward screenshots outside the company. The site is not indexed by search engines and is intended to sit behind a sign-in.

15. How is the report built and maintained?

It is a single self-contained web page. A scheduled job pulls the Salesforce changes each weekday morning, merges them into a small data file, rebuilds the page, and publishes it. The grouping rules and colors are part of the page's code, which is kept in a private code repository along with a technical README and a step-by-step refresh runbook.

If you have an idea for a new tab, status, or column, or notice a rule that no longer matches how the team works, send it to the report owner.
